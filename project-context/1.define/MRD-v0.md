# Community app — market/problem discovery brief v0

Date: 2026-11-08. Status: discovery-stage MRD draft, not validated market requirements.
This document separates reported observations from product hypotheses. It is not external market research.
No market size, competitive feature audit, pricing research or attendee-demand study has been completed.

## 1. Target segment and desired progress
Chosen audience: Christian entrepreneurs seeking fellow entrepreneurs for support and community.
Primary intended progress: build an ongoing supportive peer relationship.
Secondary progress: find relevant, willing business coaching/mentorship that takes faith seriously.
The proposed value is not merely a list of people sharing a label or a source of authoritative religious answers.
Geographic market, initial cohort boundaries, eligibility and group-size/cadence are not yet selected.
No inference is made about the project owner's faith or the faith of existing coffee-meetup attendees.

## 2. What has actually been reported
The organiser reports that coffee-meetup regulars discuss topics of interest and experience;
an AI-investing event emerged this way. A speaker shared it with friends, and a cafe partner
shared it with their community. An entrance fee included coffee/a drink.
Interested attendees privately message the organiser, who records and manually counts them.
The cafe arrangement was described as flexible and generally fine; no urgent capacity pain was established.
Some people exchange LinkedIn/contact connections and may meet independently. That follow-up
was described as not typical. Repeated events still build familiarity through seeing the same faces.
These are qualitative organiser reports, not independently observed events or measured conversion rates.

## 3. Hypotheses, not findings
H1. Some attendees want to reconnect but missed exchanging contacts before leaving.
    Basis: organiser intuition; attendees have not been asked about this need in the described work.
H2. Private reciprocal requests lead to mutually wanted connections and later catch-ups.
    Basis: an appealing design hypothesis; no real-user trial has run.
H3. Returning to a circle/familiar people can create perceived support, not just recognition.
    Basis: familiarity was observed; supportive outcomes were not measured.
H4. Shared challenges/interests plus compatible meeting preferences improve connection quality.
    Basis: agreed design reasoning; no comparison data or settled ranking criteria.
H5. A faith-centred context adds useful differentiation beyond existing social/business communities.
    Basis: the chosen product audience and proposed stewardship/encouragement needs; demand remains untested.

Fragmentation across churches/business networks is a working hypothesis, not a counted market fact.
Continually adding people is not an established acquisition engine or retention mechanism.
A preference for group familiarity is valid; lack of 1:1 meetings is not automatically a problem to fix.

## 4. Existing alternatives and inspiration
Reported current tools/workarounds: WhatsApp private messages, organiser records/manual counts,
LinkedIn/contact exchange, recurring coffee gatherings and speaker/cafe community distribution.
We have not established that these alternatives are inadequate or that participants want another app.

The owner referenced Timeleft, Lectures After Dark, Meetup and Luma, alongside blind networking
and richer gathering formats. These are inspiration/future integration references, not researched
competitor capability claims. Blind networking is not assumed to mean romantic matchmaking.
No direct publishing, booking or payment API availability is asserted for any provider.

## 5. First inexpensive demand experiment
Proposed trial: at an existing gathering, the owner/organisers offer a voluntary post-event
reconnection action, with transparent discoverability and private bilateral permission.
A manual workflow can precede substantial application investment. This is proposed, not scheduled.
Do not publish an attendee list or share contact details just because someone attended.
The agent has not been authorised to contact participants or venue partners.

Observe opportunities actually offered, opt-ins, distinct requests, reciprocal acceptance,
and optionally reported later catch-ups. Keep people, requests and pairs separate.
Set the observation window, cohort definition and keep/change/stop criteria before collecting results;
those choices are still open. Missing reports are not evidence of failed or successful catch-ups.
A small voluntary pilot provides directional evidence and selection effects, not population-level
market proof or causal attribution. Ask why people acted or declined without collecting intimate histories.

Possible interpretations to investigate, not conclusions:
- Low opt-in: unclear value, privacy concern, timing or genuinely low need.
- Opt-in but no requests: discovery/availability issues or no relevant person present.
- Requests but low acceptance: fit, trust, unwanted approaches or response burden.
- Acceptance but no follow-up: coordination friction or insufficient ongoing value.

## 6. Evidence that would change direction
Continue or improve the flow if people voluntarily use it and both parties report meaningful value.
Simplify to recurring circles/events if familiarity is what participants primarily want.
Change discoverability or contact controls if discomfort or unwanted requests emerge.
Do not intensify recruitment or engagement pressure merely to improve a funnel number.
Business-coaching features require separate relevance, willingness and expectation evidence.

## 7. Commercial and supply unknowns
No platform business model, willingness to pay or monetisation choice has been agreed.
A cafe entrance fee is not evidence of willingness to pay for the platform.
Initial organiser supply exists in the owner's network, but workload/capacity is unmeasured.
Venue inventory, confirmed-reservation responsibilities and payment operations remain future research.
No segment count, total addressable market, acquisition cost, retention baseline or launch date is asserted.

## 8. Relation to the learning project
A reversible synthetic prototype is worthwhile for learning even before product demand is proven.
Its automated tests validate specified mechanics, not market need or comprehensive security.
Separate technical verification from voluntary behavioural evidence; iterate both without conflating them.
